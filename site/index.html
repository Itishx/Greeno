When he shows up on his own

Three moments. Exactly three.

A fourth is how this becomes annoying software, so there is no fourth timer anywhere in the app. Everything else he does, he does because you asked.

01

Morning greet

The notch drops at your own start time, once. Your calendar, the overnight mail that actually needs you, what is due, and only then the thing you said you cared about. Day-stuff leads. Goals are a clause at the end, never a scorecard.

Inside a 45 minute window after your start time

02

Stuck nudge

He glances at the screen and mostly says nothing. When he does have something, the notch reads "Greeno has a suggestion" and waits for you to open it. Suggestions are read, never spoken at you. Then he stands down.

Event driven, with a 30 minute floor between nudges

03

Night debrief

"How did it go?" You talk for about two minutes, the notebook fills in live on screen while you do, he reacts to what actually happened, and the day closes.

Your evening, on your say-so

Nudges fire on events, never on blind timers. A timer that fires while you are deep in something is the single fastest way to get uninstalled.

When you call him

Double-Option, and just talk.

Tap Option twice and a live voice lane opens. You can talk over him and he stops mid-word. These are not features on a shelf, they are what he reaches for while doing the job.

Chat with what is in front of you

The page, the PDF, the video. He already has it open, so you do not paste anything anywhere.

Notes from anything you listen to

Lectures, meetings, a long call. In goes the audio, out comes a clean summary you can actually read afterwards.

Hera, real-time dictation

System wide

Silent dictation into any caret on the Mac. It cleans itself in chunks in the background while you keep talking.

Routines, not chores

"Triage my inbox." "What is the news." Say it once and it becomes a thing that happens, rather than a thing you remember.

Focus mode

Notifications die on the Mac and on your iPhone, because it runs through a real Shortcut and Focus syncs itself across your devices. Music starts. The timer runs.

Delegate a one-off

Anything that is a job rather than a conversation. He takes it, does it, and tells you it is done.

The first ten seconds

He arrives before the app does.

Nothing opens on your screen until he has introduced himself. No window, no form, no checklist waiting behind him. The notch traces itself, his name resolves one letter at a time, and then the word collapses into the width of his eyes and he is looking at you.

Out loud, in his own voice: "Hi, I am Greeno. I am here to hold you accountable." Then he climbs out of the notch and the app opens behind him.

Talking to him

He stops mid-word the moment you start.

Interrupting him is not an edge case that got handled. It is the thing the whole voice pipeline was built around, because anything you cannot talk over is not a conversation.

Option then Option opens and closes the lane
  • Barge-in is instant The moment your voice hits the mic, his current sentence is killed. Not at the end of the sentence. Now.
  • His voice is his, not a preset Turn-taking and transcription come from one system. The voice comes from another, on purpose, because a generic assistant voice would take the character with it.
  • He knows which mic you are on A headset at your mouth and a laptop mic across the desk get treated differently, or the quiet half of what you said gets scrubbed as room noise.
  • Short, even in the heavy moments A real reaction does not get wordier because the moment is big. It gets realer.

What he can do to your Mac

A closed list. Nothing else, ever.

This is the whole list. It is enforced in code rather than asked for in a prompt, and there is a test that fails if anybody tries to add to it.

focus.on focus.off app.open app.quit url.open music.play music.pause shortcut.run send a message send mail place a call

Nothing on the list can contact a person

He will remind you to text your mum. He will never text her. Chores get automated. Relationships do not.

Nothing runs until you say yes

Screen watching is opt-in and visibly armed. Quitting an app and running a Shortcut ask every time, because closing someone's unsaved work is not undoable.

Product law

The six rules he is not allowed to break.

  • He speaks first, or he is nothing.Something that waits to be opened is a tool, and there are enough of those.
  • Suggestions are read, never spoken at you.Being talked at while you are working is the opposite of help.
  • Mornings lead with the day.Your calendar first. Goals are a footnote, not a report card.
  • Chores get automated. Relationships never do.He reminds you to text your mum. He never texts her.
  • Nothing runs on your machine until you say yes.Every permission is tied to one job he cannot do without it.
  • Say only what is needed.A line or two beats a paragraph. He never pads and he never repeats you back to yourself.

Straight answers

Before you ask.

Can I use it today?

No. Greeno is pre-release and there is no public build yet. Early access goes out in small batches to people who are willing to actually talk to him for a week and tell us where he was wrong.

Is he watching my screen?

Only if you turn that on, and it is visibly armed when it is running. The stuck nudge takes a glance, decides almost every time to say nothing, and cannot speak again for at least thirty minutes even when it does have something.

Can he message people for me?

No, and that is a design decision rather than a gap. There is no send, no message, no mail and no call anywhere on the list of things he is able to do. A test fails if anyone adds one.

What does he need from my Mac?

The microphone, or nothing works at all. Accessibility, for the double-Option shortcut. Screen Recording, only if you want the stuck nudge. Automation, so he can start a focus, open an app or change the music. Each one is asked for in the moment he needs it, tied to the job it does.

Is he going to nag me?

There are three moments and there will not be a fourth. Two of them you can move or switch off, and the third only ever renders quietly in the notch and waits for you to open it.

What happens to what I tell him?

It goes into your notebook, in your own words, and you can edit or delete any line of it. The notebook is the thing he reads before he says anything, so editing it changes him.

Make every day feel a little less boring.

Leave an address and we will bring you in with the next batch. Small batches, because the point is to hear back from you.
